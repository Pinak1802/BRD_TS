## 1. Document Purpose and Business Context

This Business Requirements Document (BRD) translates the supplied Compact Business Vision into a structured, verifiable set of business requirements for the **Pension Scheme Administration Platform** (the "Platform"). The Platform is intended to replace a fragmented, manual, spreadsheet-driven pension administration environment with a secure, centralized, controlled digital platform.

**Authority statement.** The supplied Business Vision is the authoritative source. It establishes the business problem, high-level capabilities, core controls, provisional success targets, scope boundaries, and decisions to confirm. Where the Vision states only a high-level capability or a proposed target, this document labels that as requiring stakeholder validation or measurement agreement. This document does **not** invent pension regulations, scheme-specific policies, financial calculations, benefit formulas, statutory deadlines, retention periods, technical architectures, or approved permission matrices.

**Scope of this document.** This BRD covers business requirements, business rules, controls, cross-cutting non-functional requirements, exception scenarios, success measures, scope boundaries, risks, dependencies, assumptions, open questions, traceability, and business acceptance considerations. It does **not** cover domain model detail, ERDs, high-level/low-level design, technical architecture, source code, or implementation plans.

**Downstream linkage.** As stated in the source vision, the intended downstream progression is: *Business Vision → BRD → Domain Model → ERD → HLD → LLD → Development & Testing*. The BRD prepares these activities by capturing business concepts and relationships at a conceptual level without prescribing identifiers, cardinalities, mandatory fields, or a database schema.

## 2. Executive Summary

The business currently administers pensions through manual, spreadsheet-driven processes with limited centralized reporting and auditability. This approach introduces risks of duplicate data entry, inconsistent records, slow reconciliation, limited operational visibility, and difficulty proving who changed or approved a transaction.

The proposed solution is a **secure, centralized digital Platform** providing controlled workflows across nine capability areas: Scheme Management; Employer Management; Member Management; Pension Accounts; Contributions and Financial Reconciliation; Investment Records; Benefits Administration; and Reporting and Audit.

**Provisional success targets** in the source vision are a 90% reduction in manual entry, 95% data accuracy, and 99.5% availability. These are treated as **provisional business targets**. Each requires an agreed definition, current baseline, measurement period, calculation method, and accountable owner before it can be used to assess delivery success.

**Key open decisions before design** include the applicable pension model (defined contribution, defined benefit, statutory, or other), the governing country/regulator and applicable scheme rules, whether the Platform records or initiates payments, supported benefit types and approvers, existing systems and integrations, migration data and retention rules, expected volumes, and security/service-level requirements.

This BRD converts the Vision's stated goals into individually identifiable, testable business requirements and rules, explicitly distinguishing confirmed needs, proposed interpretations, provisional targets, and unresolved stakeholder decisions.

### 5.1 Role-Based Access and Separation of Duties

The Vision requires **role-based access** and states that **submitting, reviewing, and approving sensitive financial or benefit actions may need to be separated**.

**Requirement: role-based access and controlled authorization must be provided.** The specific permission matrix — which roles may submit, review, approve, modify, and reconcile sensitive transactions — is an **unresolved decision** (Section 13, Question Q-09). Until agreed, the Platform shall support configurable role definitions with the capability to separate submission, review, and approval functions.

**Control objective (proposed):** where submission, review, and approval responsibilities are separated, the Platform shall not permit a single actor to complete a sensitive financial or benefit action end-to-end. **This control objective requires confirmation that separation is mandated for each sensitive action class** (business rule RULE-009 requires stakeholder confirmation of the affected action classes).

| EX-05 | Failed financial transaction | A financial action fails to complete. | RULE-007 | Failure recorded with reason and affected record | Retry policy; rollback or manual recovery |
| EX-06 | Missing or inconsistent records | Required associations (employer/member/scheme) are missing or inconsistent. | RULE-003; RULE-007 | Incomplete record flagged with missing fields | Hold vs. reject behavior |
| EX-07 | Unauthorized or unapproved changes | A change occurs without proper authorization or approval. | RULE-005; RULE-008; RULE-009 | Audit record captures actor and timestamp | Detection and remediation process |
| EX-08 | Incomplete benefit request or unsuccessful outcome | A benefit request is incomplete or is rejected. | RULE-002; RULE-007; RULE-009 | Request, validation result, and decision recorded | Feedback mechanism to the member |

**Distinction:** The "Expected Control Objective" columns state **confirmed control objectives** derived from the Vision (flag and resolve, do not silently complete). The "Unresolved Handling Decision" columns record **unapproved operational procedures** that require stakeholder confirmation before design.
